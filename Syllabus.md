# DESN 210 / BTEC 420 (Spring 2020)

## Introduction to Programming for Business Applications

#### Case Western Reserve University, Weatherhead School of Management

### Intro

The course will introduce students to the basics of programming logic utilizing the Python programming language and environment. The course will help students understand how to apply programming solutions and related algorithmic thinking to solve common business and decision problems. This class is a great introduction into programming logic, it just happens to use Python. This course will teach the fundamentals of programming logic, which could be applied to any programming language available today or into the future.

**_Attendance_**

Notecards with the names of the full roster of the class will be maintained by the instructor. The notecards will be cycled through with an accompanying question for each student. The attendance grade will be (# attempted questions) / (# given questions - 1). For example, if a student is asked 10 in class questions and attempts to answer 9 of them they will get 100% of the attendance points (9 / (10-1)). If a student attempts to answer 5 of 8 given questions the student will receive 71% of the attendance points (5 / (8-1)). You will not receive more than 100% attendance points based on question attempts alone. If you consistently provide quality answers bonus points may be given.

**_Quizzes_**

There will be four quizzes throughout the course. These quizzes are designed to measure the student’s comprehension of the material. Quizzes will be on paper and may include coding, comprehension, and multiple choice questions. Each quiz should take about 30 minutes.

**_Coding Assignments_**

Students will work independently on each programming exercise. These assignments will attempt to use various types of business datasets. Assignments might involve adjusting existing code, producing new code, producing a file in an automated fashion, or comprehension based questions. Coding assignments will be graded against a set of automated tests for correctness, partial credit will not be given. Questions-Answer type comprehension questions will be graded by the instructor, partial credit is possible on these.

**_Final Project_**

Learning to work with others in business problem resolution is critical for employers. The final project will be a group effort. Group assignments will be finalized half way through the semester and determined by the instructor. The final project has two submissions. First, the group will produce a white paper style proposal. Second, the group must submit a working program that supports their paper submission.

The paper will identify the problem, propose a solution, articulate the business requirements and specifications, include a high level flow diagram of the coding logic, and articulate the expected results of the coded solution.

The program must support the paper, produce the expected results, and the code must have comments within the final compiled project. In real world scenarios plans change as more details about the data or the problem space becomes clear. This may happen during this project so a revision to the white paper may be submitted with commentary about why the original plan was wrong.
